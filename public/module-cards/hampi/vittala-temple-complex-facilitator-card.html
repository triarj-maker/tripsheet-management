Lead Facilitator -  Responsibilities, Setup, and Safety Watch

  • Opening safety briefing: Before the guide begins the module context at the parking lot, the facilitator should give a short safety briefing covering relevant (not all) points below.
  • Lost location: If anyone is separated from the group, they should report to the security at the entrance of the Vittala Temple Complex or to the nearest available security personnel.
  • Group size: One guide and one facilitator should manage each student group maximum 20 pax.
  • Crowded temple complex: The Vijaya Vittala courtyard is often crowded. Each group must remain together inside the temple complex.
  • Headcounts: Conduct a headcount before entering the temple, at the exit of the temple, and again before boarding the battery cart.
  • Parking-to-temple movement: Students walk on a gravel and mud road shared with battery carts and occasional vehicles.
  • Walking line: Students should walk on one side of the road, ideally in single file or in a controlled line.
  • Dust control: Students should not kick dust while walking.
  • Line discipline: Students must keep up with the person in front. If one student slows down or gets distracted, the whole line can break and large gaps can form.
  • Pushkarini safety: Students are not allowed to enter the water or go close to the water edge.
  • Temple conduct: Students should not touch carvings, pillars, the Stone Chariot, musical columns, or any protected monument surface.
  • Purandara Dasa Mantapa conduct: Footwear removal is mandatory. Students must remain quiet and respectful.
  • River safety: No one may enter the water, put their feet in the water, or move toward the river edge without instruction.
  • Speaker use at Purandara Mantapa: Music may be played only if it does not disturb others. Only Purandara dasa compositions played respectfully.

Section 1 . Parking Lot Briefing

Location

Parking lot near Vijaya Vittala Temple complex.

Intent of this Section

  • Set context for the Vijaya Vittala Complex before the walk begins.
  • Establish the significance of the temple as one of the finest examples of Vijayanagara-style temple architecture.

Section 2 . Kudregombe Mantapa (Optional)

Location

Kudregombe Mantapa, along the bazaar street leading to Vijaya Vittala Temple.

Intent of this Section

  • Formally begin the module.
  • Introduce Vijaya Vittala as one of the most important and ornate temple complexes in Hampi.
  • Help students understand the scale and function of the temple bazaar.
  • Introduce Vijayanagara as a globally connected medieval city.
  • Use the horse market to connect trade, military power, Portuguese networks, and imperial success.
  • Help students see that temple spaces were also connected to economic, social, and political life.

Optional Prompts / Questions

Use these if the guide is delivering the section as an information-heavy explanation and the facilitator needs to bring students back into the discussion.

  • What kinds of things do you think would have been traded in a market like this?
  • Why would a temple need such a large market street in front of it?
  • Why do you think horses were so valuable in medieval warfare?

Section 3 . Pushkarini (Optional)

Location

Pushkarini / ritual stepwell tank near the Vijaya Vittala Temple approach.

Intent of this Section

  • Introduce the Pushkarini as both ritual infrastructure and water infrastructure.
  • Use the surrounding landscape to identify Hampi as semi-arid.
  • Show students that Vijayanagara’s urban success depended on careful planning and engineering.

Optional Prompts / Questions

Use these if the guide is delivering the section as an information-heavy explanation and the facilitator needs to bring students back into the discussion.

  • What does this vegetation tell us about the kind of landscape we are in?
  • Why would a city in a semi-arid region need such careful water planning?
  • What are the three possible sources of water for a tank like this?
  • Why do you think this tank was built in this exact location?

Section 4 . Temple Entrance Orientation

Location

Inside the eastern gopura of the Vijaya Vittala Temple complex.

Intent of this Section

  • Give students a quick spatial orientation before entering the main temple narrative.
  • Introduce Dravidian temple architecture.
  • Help students identify the major structures they will visit.

Section 5 . Kalyana Mantapa

Location

Kalyana Mantapa, inside the Vijaya Vittala Temple complex.

Intent of this Section

  • Explain stone construction, symmetry, granite use, and interlocking building methods.
  • Introduce the Yali as a mythical creature, architectural motif, and military symbol.
  • Use Yali-mounted warlords to discuss the Vijayanagara army, regional diversity, elite identity, and cosmopolitanism.
  • Help students see that temple sculpture communicates power, protection, status, and global connection.

Optional Prompts / Questions

Use these if the guide is delivering the section as an information-heavy explanation and the facilitator needs to bring students back into the discussion.

  • What animal features can you identify in the Yali?
  • Why do you think a temple pillar would show warriors or warlords?
  • What do the different clothes, weapons, and hairstyles tell us?
  • When you imagine a king, what do you usually imagine him wearing?
  • What does fashion tell us about connection between cultures?

Section 6 . Ranga Mantapa and the Musical Pillars

Location

Ranga Mantapa / 56-pillared hall inside the Vijaya Vittala Temple complex.

Intent of this Section

  • Introduce the musical pillars through evidence rather than spectacle.
  • Help students understand colonnaded pillars as a major Vijayanagara architectural innovation.
  • Explain the musical pillar claim through two major lenses: structural mechanics and geological material properties.
  • Present scientific, material, textual, artistic, archaeological, and performance-based evidence in student-friendly language.
  • Encourage students to form informed judgement rather than simply believe or dismiss the claim.
  • Connect the Ranga Mantapa to classical music, dance, ritual performance, and temple offering.

Optional Prompts / Questions

Use these if the guide is delivering the section as an information-heavy explanation and the facilitator needs to bring students back into the discussion.

  • Does anyone know what a tuning fork is?
  • Which kind of evidence do you find most convincing: structure, geology, scientific measurement, artistic evidence, textual evidence, archaeological wear marks, or the function of the hall?
  • Why should we examine evidence instead of simply accepting a claim?

Section 7 . Ranga Mantapa Mouldings and Composite Sculptures

Pathway: Heritage Focus Pathway: include. Core Temple Pathway: optional, only if time and group energy allow.

Location

Outer edge / basement mouldings of the Ranga Mantapa.

Intent of this Section

  • Offer a short visual enrichment stop.
  • Draw attention to fine architectural detailing.

Section 8 . Stone Chariot

Location

Stone Chariot inside the Vijaya Vittala Temple complex.

Intent of this Section

  • Reframe the Stone Chariot as a ritual object, not just a photo monument.
  • Explain its function as a Garuda shrine.
  • Connect it to chariot festivals, public devotion, symbolic participation, and medieval spectacle.

Optional Prompts / Questions

Use if time allows.

  • Why do you think people wanted to pull the temple chariot?
  • Why might moving the stone wheel feel meaningful to a devotee?
  • Why do you think this became the most iconic image of Hampi?

Section 9 . Purandara Dasa Mantapa and Musical Reflection

Location

Purandara Dasa Mantapa, near the Tungabhadra River behind the Vijaya Vittala Temple complex.

Intent of this Section

  • Close the module through music, landscape, quiet, and reflection.
  • Introduce Purandara Dasa as a founding father of Carnatic music.
  • Connect Vijayanagara to the systematization and spread of devotional music.
  • Allow brief journaling, sketching, doodling, or poetic expression.

Pre-entry Briefing

Before entering the space, the guide or facilitator should say:

“We are now going toward Purandara Dasa Mantapa.

Before we enter, please listen carefully.

This is a place that many people consider sacred. So we will remove our footwear before entering.

The mantapa is right on the edge of the river. The water is beautiful, but no one is allowed to get into the water, put their feet in the water, or move toward the edge.

We will sit quietly here. We will listen to a story, listen to some music if the situation allows, and spend a little time in reflection.

This is not a place for shouting, running, joking loudly, or playing near the water.

Please remain quiet and respectful.”
