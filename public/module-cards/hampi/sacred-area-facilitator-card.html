Lead Facilitator -  Responsibilities, Setup, and Safety Watch

  • Coordinate before starting: Confirm the pathway, optional sections, timing, route, and whether Virupaksha Temple is included.
  • Set movement structure: Create groups for every 40 students. Assign one adult to lead and one to trail.
  • Brief students: Stay with the group, no running, carry water, keep food packed away, and go to Virupaksha Temple entrance if separated.
  • Track essentials: Confirm student count, teacher count, water, caps, first-aid kit, and support facilitator positions.
  • Manage guide delivery: Keep the guide student-facing. Step in if narration becomes too long, abstract, or disconnected from what students can observe.
  • Use recovery prompts: Ask short questions when students become passive after an information-heavy explanation.
  • Make live pathway decisions: Choose Section 5, 6, 7, or 8 based on time, weather, student energy, and school expectations.
  • Watch safety: Control movement on stone, steps, slopes, and open rock. Prioritize shade, hydration, monkey awareness, and respectful conduct at Virupaksha.
  • Close with meaning: Ensure the module ends with a clear takeaway, reflection, sunset pause, or living-heritage observation.

Section 1 . Sacred Area Introduction

Location

Entrance gate near Sasivekalu Ganesha, beside the large Sacred Area map.

Intent of this Section

  • Orient students to the Sacred Area as one of the most important zones within Vijayanagara.
  • Help students understand the scale of Vijayanagara as a major pre-modern city and imperial capital.
  • Connect Hampi to larger histories of trade, globalization, European arrival, and South Indian cultural change.
  • Use the map to locate the Tungabhadra River, temple clusters, hills, and the main temple complexes.
  • Establish that the Sacred Area is not one monument, but a large religious, cultural, and historical landscape.

Optional Prompts / Questions

Use these if the guide is delivering the section as an information-heavy explanation and the facilitator needs to bring students back into the discussion. Do it whenever appropriate in between or at the end.

  • Why do you think this place has that name - Sacred Area?  (After intro)
  • Does anyone know which were the two largest city-states in the world during 1500s ? (After Intro)

Section 2 . Sasivekalu Ganesha

Location

In front of Sasivekalu Ganesha monolith.

Intent of this Section

  • Introduce students to Hampi’s granite landscape and the use of local material in architecture and sculpture.
  • Explain what a monolith is using Sasivekalu Ganesha as a visible example.
  • Introduce local naming traditions and the difference between formal history and local usage.
  • Introduce Shilpa Shastra, the Tala system, and the idea that Indian sculpture is governed by proportion, symbolism, and emotional intent.
  • Help students see sculpture not only as representation, but as designed experience.

Optional Prompts / Questions

Use these if the guide is delivering the section as an information-heavy explanation and the facilitator needs to bring students back into the discussion. Do it whenever appropriate in between or at the end.

  • If you look around, there is one material that is everywhere. Underneath your feet, all around you, fort walls are made of it, pillars and roofs are made of it.  What is this material?  (Infront of Ganesha)
  • What tells us that this sculpture was made from a single piece of stone? (Infront of Ganesha)  

Section 3 . Why Vijayanagara Chose This Location

Location

Hemakuta Hill, seated near the double-storied mantapa, with landscape view.

Intent of this Section

  • Shift students from passive listening into historical and geographical reasoning.
  • Use the landscape as evidence to ask why Vijayanagara may have chosen this location as its capital.
  • Help students infer strategic, ecological, political, and sacred reasons from what they can observe.
  • Establish that capital cities are shaped by multiple factors, not only by kings or single events.
  • Prepare students to compare evidence-based historical reasoning with royal origin narratives.

Optional Prompts / Questions

Use these if the guide is delivering the section as an information-heavy explanation and the facilitator needs to bring students back into the discussion. Do it whenever appropriate in between or at the end.

  • What do you see in the landscape that might make this a good place for a capital city? (Once seated near double storied mantapa)
  • Why might building near an existing sacred place help a new empire gain support?
  • Why would Vijayanagara choose to be near its northern frontier instead of hiding deep in the south?

Section 4 . Origin Narrative of Vijayanagara . Vidyaranya Narrative

Location

Same seated space on Hemakuta Hill.

Intent of this Section

  • Introduce the literary/royal origin story of Vijayanagara.
  • Help students understand how empires use stories to create legitimacy, meaning, and sacred authority.
  • Contrast practical historical reasoning with symbolic narrative.
  • Show that origin stories may reveal what rulers wanted people to believe, even when they are not straightforward historical explanations.
  • Help students understand divine ordination as a common political tool used by kings and dynasties to justify rule.
  • Bring out the subtle political messaging in the Vidyaranya narrative: Vijayanagara as a divinely sanctioned kingdom created to protect dharma and the dharmic way of life.
  • Encourage students to compare narratives rather than simply accept or reject them.

Optional Prompts / Questions

Use these if the guide is delivering the section as an information-heavy explanation and the facilitator needs to bring students back into the discussion. Do it whenever appropriate in between or at the end.

  • Why do you think this became the most popular origin story of Vijayanagara? (at the end)
  • How does Vidyaranya’s presence make the founding of Vijayanagara feel divinely approved? (at the end)
  • Which explanation helps us understand the capital better: the geographical explanation, the origin story, or both together?

Section 5 . Pampa Mahatmya and Sacred Landscape (Optional)

Location

Hemakuta Hill, seated at the top overlooking Virupaksha Temple and the surrounding sacred landscape.

Intent of this Section

  • Introduce Hemakuta, Virupaksha, and Pampa as part of a sacred landscape that predates Vijayanagara.
  • Help students understand the Pampa Mahatmya as a sacred place-text, not merely as a story.
  • Narrate the sacred origin story of Pampakshetra in a respectful and engaging way.
  • Connect the physical landscape of Hemakuta, the river, the lake, and Virupaksha Temple to the Pampa-Shiva narrative.
  • Clearly separate the mythological/literary perspective from the archaeological/historical perspective.
  • Explain how older local goddess traditions, Shaivism, pilgrimage, literature, festivals, and temple worship became layered together over time.
  • Help students see sacred geography as something created through story, ritual, memory, landscape, and community practice.

Optional Prompts / Questions

Use these if the guide delivers this section as an information-heavy explanation and the facilitator needs to help students connect the story, the landscape, and the historical layering.

  • What is the difference between the Pampa Mahatmya story and the archaeological/historical sequence?
  • Why might an older local goddess tradition become connected to a larger Shiva tradition over time?
  • What does the Pampa-Virupaksha festival tell us about community life and cultural continuity in Hampi?

Section 6 . Temples as Community, Economic, and Political Centers . (Optional)

Location

Near Mula Virupaksha, with a bird’s-eye view of Virupaksha Temple and the surrounding landscape.

Intent of this Section

  • Use the bird’s-eye view to show how the temple sits within a larger urban and sacred system.
  • Explain that temples in South India were not only places of worship, but institutions of economy, community, administration, charity, and power.
  • Connect temple markets, toll collection, kitchens, legal mediation, festivals, and social life.
  • Help students understand temple-centered urbanism in Vijayanagara and wider South India.
  • Prepare students to see Virupaksha as a living institution, not just an architectural monument.

Optional Prompts / Questions

Use these if the guide is delivering the section as an information-heavy explanation and the facilitator needs to bring students back into the discussion. Do it whenever appropriate in between or at the end.

  • Why would markets form in front of major temples?
  • What roles did temples play beyond worship?
  • How are traders, farmers, priests, pilgrims, and local communities all connected through the temple?

Section 7 . Sunset Close (Optional)

Location

Hemakuta sunset viewpoint.

Intent of this Section

  • Provide emotional closure to the Sacred Area walk.
  • Allow students to sit with the landscape after the narration-heavy sections.
  • Create a quiet moment of reflection rather than ending abruptly with information.

Section 8 . Virupaksha Temple Close . Optional

Location

Virupaksha Temple.

Intent of this Section

  • Shift students into respectful observation of a living sacred space.
  • Help students experience Virupaksha Temple not only as a monument, but as an active place of worship.
  • Reinforce the distinction between archaeological ruins and living heritage.
  • Close the walk with embodied experience rather than only narration.

Module Has Landed If

The Sacred Area Heritage Walk has landed if students can:

  • Explain why this part of Hampi is called the Sacred Area.
  • Understand that Vijayanagara was a major imperial capital, not just a ruin site.
  • Identify at least three reasons why Hampi was suitable as a capital: boulder hills, Tungabhadra River, granite, sacred geography, frontier location, or local political connections.
  • Recognize that the origin narrative of Vijayanagara is not only a story, but also a way of creating legitimacy and sacred authority.
  • Distinguish between evidence-based historical reasoning and mythological / literary narratives.
  • Understand that temples in medieval South India were not only places of worship, but also centers of economy, community, power, and social life.
  • See Hampi as a layered landscape: sacred, political, historical, artistic, geographical, and living.
