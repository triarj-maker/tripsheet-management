Mock Excavation - Facilitator Card

Mock Excavation Facilitator Card

Live-Use Reference

Use During Live Module

Use this card as a live execution reminder. It is not a training document. For detailed rationale, scoring guidance and pit background, refer to the full Mock Excavation Facilitator Playbook.

Snapshot

Duration: Approximately 120 minutes, with up to 10 additional minutes for excavation catch-up.

Batch: 25–50 students.

Teams: 4, one team per pit.

Adult Support: 1 school teacher / staff member per pit.

Pit: 1 ft × 6 ft, divided into 4 quarters.

Learning Flow: Excavate → Record → Study Artefacts → Notice Patterns → Build Claims → Interpret → Brief.

Start-of-Module Checks

Reach the site at least 30 minutes before students arrive.

• Worksheets ready in required numbers: Excavation & Recording, Artefact Case Files, correct Site Interpretation sheet for each pit.

• Pens / pencils and spares ready. Scoring sheet / score-recording method ready.

• All four pits prepared. Check tents, tables, chairs, flags, chalk boundaries, quarter markings, tools and artefact tables.

• Toilets physically checked for water and cleanliness. Toilet paper, soap, sanitiser, tissues and dustbins in place. Drinking water and first-aid available.

• Confirm one school teacher / staff member per pit. If setup is incomplete, escalate immediately: Nagraj → Basava → Arjun.

Live Flow

1. Opening and Team Setup

Expected Duration: 5–10 minutes opening + 3–5 minutes team setup.

Facilitator Sequence

• Allow toilet / water use if needed, then bring the full batch together.

• Refresh the key idea: protect and record evidence before interpreting it.

• Confirm four teams, one teacher / staff member per team, and one Student Group Leader per team.

• Send teams to pits, hand over the Excavation & Recording worksheet and assign first-quarter roles.

• Explain that roles rotate after every quarter and that the activity is scored out of 30.

Suggested Framing

“Your first job is not to guess the story. Protect and record the evidence first. By the end, your team should be able to explain what you think happened here and what evidence supports that interpretation.”

Learning Anchor

A guess is not yet an answer. Claims need evidence.

2. Excavation

Expected Duration: 60 minutes. Allow up to 10 additional minutes only if needed.

Excavation Order

Quarter 1 → Quarter 2 → Quarter 3 → Quarter 4

A quarter is complete only after finds are carefully exposed, measured, recorded, removed, gently cleaned and placed on the artefact table.

Watch For

• Pit-boundary discipline and careful digging. No stepping across the pit, stabbing, forceful scraping or rushing.

• Measure and record before removal. Soil must be checked before disposal.

• Teacher / staff rotates roles after each completed quarter.

• All excavation functions remain covered: Excavators, Soil Screeners, Measurers, Record Keeper(s), Table Manager(s).

• Students remain engaged. Unfamiliar objects may be recorded by description or serial number. Identification can come later.

Progress Checks

• 30 min: Check every pit. If behind, identify the bottleneck and improve pace without taking over.

• 45 min: Check again. Tell delayed teams how much time remains and remove avoidable delays.

• 60 min: Allow up to 10 extra minutes where required.

• After catch-up: If artefacts remain buried, intervene and extract them quickly. Do not allow excavation to consume synthesis time.

Useful Prompts

“What has to happen before that object can be removed?”

“Has its position been measured and recorded?”

“Is this quarter actually complete?”

“Who is responsible for that task in your current role?”

3. Table Display and Excavation Close

Expected Duration: 2–3 minutes.

Before moving on, check that all recovered artefacts are on the table, gently cleaned and orderly; the recording sheet is complete; unrelated items are off the table.

Score the Table Display at this point.

Transition

“Tools down. Until now your job was to uncover, measure, record, protect and organise the evidence. Now we move from finding objects to studying what they might tell us.”

4. Short Reset

Expected Duration: Approximately 10 minutes.

Stagger the break as teams finish excavation. Coconut water / water, toilet use if needed, hand washing or sanitiser. Keep students near their pit team and artefact table. Do not let the reset become a long break.

5. Artefact Case File

Expected Duration: 10–15 minutes.

Facilitator Sequence

• Decide whether students work individually or in pairs based on age, cognitive ability, language and group readiness.

• Assign one artefact to each student or pair. Keep artefacts at the pit table.

• Students first describe and sketch what they observe, then infer possible use and what the artefact may reveal.

• Keep this stage focused on the individual artefact. Do not allow the team to build the full pit story yet.

Useful Prompts

“What can you actually see?”

“What about the object makes you think that?”

“Is that an observation or an inference?”

“What are you still unsure about?”

Learning Anchor

Observation first. Inference second.

6. Artefact Identification Check

Expected Duration: Approximately 30 seconds per team.

Quickly scan each artefact table after the Case Files. If a traditional object is unfamiliar, clarify only its basic identity or established practical function.

Examples: agricultural sickles, winnowing baskets, cattle decorations, ritual implements, manuscript / stylus, betel-related objects.

Key Rule

Give specialist identification where needed. Do not give the interpretation.

7. Group Site Interpretation

Expected Duration: Approximately 30 minutes.

Worksheet Flow

Investigation Brief → Context Clue → Patterns We Notice → Claims & Evidence → Our Interpretation → Archaeological Briefing Plan

Facilitator Sequence

• Protect this time. This is the main learning landing point of the module.

• Teacher / staff keeps the team together, encourages discussion before writing and brings quieter students into the conversation.

• Facilitator circulates between teams and uses prompts that return students to the evidence.

• Do not supply claims, interpretations or the final pit story.

Useful Prompts

“What pattern do you notice?”

“Which artefacts seem connected?”

“What evidence supports that claim?”

“What else could explain it?”

“How confident are you?”

“Can you make that claim from what is actually present?”

Critical Rule

Interpret from what is present at the site. Do not treat something that is missing as proof that it never existed.

8. Final Archaeological Briefings

Expected Duration: 3 minutes per team, plus transitions.

Location: Onsite by default.

Each team chooses 2 or 3 speakers.

The Briefing Should

• Present the team’s strongest claims.

• Explain the evidence supporting each claim.

• Avoid listing every artefact.

• Use careful language where certainty is limited.

Recommended Structure

“We think ___ because we found / noticed ___.”

If Needed, Prompt With

“What is your strongest claim?”

“Which evidence best supports it?”

“What are you still uncertain about?”

9. Closing

Keep the close brief.

Evidence → Patterns → Claims → Interpretation

Suggested Closing Line

“Archaeology did not happen simply when you found the objects. It happened when you recorded them, compared them, noticed patterns, argued from evidence and decided how much the evidence allowed you to claim.”

Scoring

Total: 30 points.

Excavation Discipline: 10

Careful excavation, pit-boundary discipline, role rotation, correct quarter sequence, recording before removal and team coordination.

Table Display: 5

Artefacts gently cleaned, neatly arranged, easy to inspect and free of unrelated clutter.

Group Synthesis Worksheet: 10

Pattern recognition, evidence-based claims, reasonable inference, careful uncertainty and meaningful completion.

Final Archaeological Briefing: 5

Strong claims, clear evidence connection, clarity of communication and appropriate uncertainty.

Critical Facilitation Rules

• Observation comes before inference. Claims must connect to evidence.

• Do not give away the pit story, even when students are struggling.

• Do identify unfamiliar specialist objects when prior cultural knowledge is required.

• Protect synthesis time. Excavation should never overrun at the expense of interpretation.

• Do not reward confidence without evidence. Uncertainty is acceptable.

• Teachers / staff facilitate pace, participation and behaviour. They do not interpret for students.

Pit Interpretation Quick Reference

Use only as facilitator background. Do not read this out to students.

Pit 01: Kitchen / Modest Household

Likely Direction: A modest household kitchen in a dry landscape outside the capital.

Evidence Cues: Terracotta, wood, coconut shell and stone; grain and cooking vessels; varied cooking tools; chillies as a chronological clue after new trade contacts in the 1500s.

Caution: Do not claim poverty simply because metal utensils are absent.

Pit 02: Religious / Priest’s Household

Likely Direction: A private ritual space associated with a learned Vaishnavite or priestly household connected to the temple world.

Evidence Cues: Gopala Krishna and Vaishnavite objects; ritual vessels and implements; manuscript and stylus; copper, brass and bronze objects; Context Clue.

Caution: Clarify specialist objects where needed, but do not supply the household interpretation.

Pit 03: Dancer / Performer

Likely Direction: The personal space or residence of a wealthy professional performer connected to temple and elite cultural networks.

Evidence Cues: Gejje / ghungroo, cymbals, jewellery, grooming objects, Nataraja and lamp, non-local objects.

Caution: Wealth does not equal royalty. Do not turn the interpretation into “queen” or “princess.”

Pit 04: Farmer / Rice-Growing Household

Likely Direction: A rice-growing household near Anegundi, potentially around a seasonal harvest-related celebration and connected to Vijayanagara’s wider economy.

Evidence Cues: Rice-harvesting sickle, winnowing basket, husked rice and vessels; differently shaped sickles; repeated cattle decorations; painted pot and clay lamp; Context Clue linking rural production to city food needs and markets.

Caution: Keep the festival name ambiguous. The evidence supports a seasonal or harvest-related celebration, not a definite named festival.
