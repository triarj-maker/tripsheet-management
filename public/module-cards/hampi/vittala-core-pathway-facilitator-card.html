Vittala - Core Pathway - Facilitator Card

Use During Live Module

Live execution card. Use only for safety, movement, timing, prompts, and reflection. For depth, read the master playbook before the trip.

Snapshot

Duration: 120 mins
Best time: 08:30 AM or 03:00 PM
Group: 1 guide + 1 facilitator per max 20 students
Route: Parking → buggy / battery cart → temple entrance → Kalyana Mantapa → Ranga Mantapa → Stone Chariot → Purandara Dasa Mantapa → exit

Use when: Vittala is one module within a larger Hampi program, or when the group is younger, tired, heat-affected, or time-constrained.

Start Checks

Pathway: Core Pathway confirmed. Buggy / battery cart movement confirmed.

Group: Water checked. Heat and fatigue checked. Extra-supervision students identified.

Adults: Teachers briefed to help keep group together.

Risk points: Buggy boarding, temple crowding, musical pillars, Stone Chariot, river edge.

Lost student: Go to Vittala entrance security or nearest security personnel.

Rules to Repeat as Needed

Stay together. No wandering.

Board and exit buggy calmly. No standing, leaning out, or changing seats.

No touching, leaning, climbing, tapping, or bags on monuments.

No testing musical pillars.

No going near, entering, or touching water.

At Purandara Dasa Mantapa: footwear off, quiet conduct.

1. Parking + Buggy Boarding

Time: 5-10 mins
Guide covers: Vittala as major temple complex. Stone Chariot + musical pillars as highlights.

Facilitator does: Headcount. Brief buggy conduct. Divide students if needed. Ensure adults visible across buggies. Gather group immediately after getting down.

2. Temple Entrance

Time: 2 mins
Guide covers: Quick layout: gopura, Kalyana Mantapa, Stone Chariot, Ranga Mantapa, sanctum.

Facilitator does: Headcount before entry. Keep group compact and out of visitor flow. Do not let students enter ahead.

3. Kalyana Mantapa

Time: 15 mins
Guide covers: Wedding hall, symmetry, granite, Yali, warlords, power, diversity, global links.

Facilitator does: Position students where Yali is visible. Stop touching or leaning immediately. Use prompt if attention drops.

Facilitator Prompts (ask if needed):

  • What animal features can you identify in the Yali?
  • Why do you think a temple pillar would show warriors or warlords?
  • What do the different clothes, weapons, and hairstyles tell us?
  • When you imagine a king, what do you usually imagine him wearing?
  • What does fashion tell us about connection between cultures?

4. Ranga Mantapa / Musical Pillars

Time: 15 mins
Guide covers: Musical pillars through evidence: structure, stone, craft, science, wear marks, performance.

Facilitator does: Stand near likely touch points. Prevent touching, tapping, striking, or testing. Keep students from crowding columns. Reinforce: this is not a demo.

Facilitator Prompts (ask if needed):

  • Does anyone know what a tuning fork is?
  • Have you seen a xylophone?
  • What is the difference between a xylophone and a lithophone?
  • Why do you think the size and shape of a column might affect its sound?
  • Why does it matter that these small columns are carved from the same stone as the main pillar?
  • Which kind of evidence do you find most convincing: structure, geology, scientific measurement, artistic evidence, textual evidence, archaeological wear marks, or the function of the hall?
  • Why should we examine evidence instead of simply accepting a claim?

5. Stone Chariot

Time: 5 mins
Guide covers: Garuda shrine, chariot festival, public devotion, symbolic participation.

Facilitator does: Set photo boundary before students move in. No touching, leaning, climbing, hands, bags, or bottles on chariot. Finish photo quickly and regroup.

Facilitator Prompts (ask if needed):

  • Why do you think people wanted to pull the temple chariot?
  • Why might moving the stone wheel feel meaningful to a devotee?
  • Why do you think this became the most iconic image of Hampi?

6. Purandara Dasa Mantapa

Time: 25-30 mins
Guide covers: Purandara Dasa, Carnatic music, devotional access, cultural continuity, quiet listening.

Facilitator decides: Cut short or skip if late, hot, restless, unsafe, crowded, disruptive, or reflection is not feasible.

Before entry, say: Sacred space. Remove footwear. Sit quietly. No loud joking or running. No river edge. No touching or entering water. Listen first, then reflect.

During guide/music: Watch river movement, talking, joking, scattered belongings.

Reflection, say: Journal now. Not note-taking. Sketch, doodle, write lines, or poem. Capture one sound, image, moment, or thought. No right answer.

Close: End before restlessness. Check footwear, belongings, and headcount.

Core Pathway Has Landed If

Students can say that Vijaya Vittala is more than a beautiful monument:

  • It combines temple architecture, ritual, power, craft, music, and living traditions.
  • Kalyana Mantapa shows protection, diversity, and imperial identity.
  • Musical pillars show the link between stone, science, craft, and performance.
  • Stone Chariot is a Garuda shrine and ritual object, not just a photo monument.
  • Purandara Dasa Mantapa connects Vijayanagara to music, devotion, and continuity.
