Sacred Area - Core Pathway - Facilitator Card

Use During Live Module

Live facilitator reference for sequence, group movement, safety, student engagement, key prompts, transitions, and what must land. Read the master playbook before the trip for full depth and script training.

Snapshot

Duration: 120 mins
Best time: 03:00 PM to 03:30 PM start
Route: Sacred Area map near Sasivekalu Ganesha → Sasivekalu Ganesha → Hemakuta Hill → Mula Virupaksha viewpoint → Hemakuta sunset viewpoint
Use when: The group includes mixed nationalities, students with limited context of Indian religious-cultural narratives, or when the walk should end with sunset and downtime rather than a temple visit.

Core Arc

The Sacred Area is not just a cluster of temples. It is a layered landscape where geography, sacred memory, political power, origin stories, temple institutions, and living cultural traditions come together.

Facilitator Rules

Keep movement tight and safe. Hemakuta has uneven stone, slopes, steps, and open rock areas.

Use prompts only when needed. Take 1-2 answers and move forward.

Do not let the discussion become a debate about whether a story is “true” or “false.” Keep focus on what stories communicate and why they matter.

Use shade wherever possible. Enforce water breaks before and after the Hemakuta climb.

At sunset, slow down. The tone should become quieter and more reflective.

1. Sacred Area Introduction

Time: 10 mins
Location: Entrance gate near Sasivekalu Ganesha, beside the large Sacred Area map

Guide narrative:
The guide introduces the Sacred Area as one of Vijayanagara’s most important religious, cultural, and historical zones. Students should understand the scale of Vijayanagara as a major pre-modern imperial capital, and locate the Tungabhadra River, temple clusters, hills, and major temple complexes on the map. The section should land that the Sacred Area is not one monument, but a larger landscape shaped by religion, politics, trade, and cultural change.

Facilitator action:

  • Gather students close to the map before the guide begins.
  • Keep the group compact so students can see the map and hear clearly.
  • Support quick responses when the guide asks questions.

Facilitator prompts (ask if needed):

  • Why do you think this place has that name - Sacred Area? (After intro)
  • Does anyone know which were the two largest city-states in the world during 1500s ? (After Intro)

Must land: Students should understand that the Sacred Area is a large sacred and historical landscape within Vijayanagara, not just a single temple or monument.

2. Sasivekalu Ganesha

Time: 10 mins
Location: In front of Sasivekalu Ganesha monolith

Guide narrative:
The guide uses Sasivekalu Ganesha to introduce monolithic sculpture, granite as Hampi’s dominant material, and local naming traditions. The section also introduces Indian sculptural ideas such as Shilpa Shastra, Tala proportions, rasa, and emotional effect. Students should see the sculpture not only as an image of Ganesha, but as a designed experience of warmth, welcome, and approachability.

Facilitator action:

  • Position students where they can see the sculpture without crowding the monument.
  • Regroup tightly before beginning the climb to Hemakuta.

Facilitator prompts (ask if needed):

  • If you look around, there is one material that is everywhere. Underneath your feet, all around you, fort walls are made of it, pillars and roofs are made of it. What is this material? (Infront of Ganesha)
  • What tells us that this sculpture was made from a single piece of stone? (Infront of Ganesha)

Must land: Sasivekalu Ganesha shows how material, craft, proportion, symbolism, and emotion come together in Hampi’s sculpture.

3. Why Vijayanagara Chose This Location

Time: 15 mins
Location: Hemakuta Hill, seated near the double-storied mantapa, with landscape view

Guide narrative:
The guide shifts students into historical and geographical reasoning by asking why Vijayanagara may have been founded here. Students should use the visible landscape as evidence: boulder hills, granite, the Tungabhadra River, older sacred geography, local political connections, and the northern frontier. The section should land that capital cities are shaped by multiple strategic, ecological, political, and sacred factors.

Facilitator action:

  • Seat students in shade if possible, with a clear landscape view.
  • Encourage observation before answers.
  • Keep responses short so the section does not overrun.
  • Use a water break before or after this section depending on heat and energy.

Facilitator prompts (ask if needed):

  • What do you see in the landscape that might make this a good place for a capital city? (Once seated near double storied mantapa)
  • Why might building near an existing sacred place help a new empire gain support?
  • Why would Vijayanagara choose to be near its northern frontier instead of hiding deep in the south?

Must land: Students should understand that Vijayanagara’s location can be explained through landscape evidence, not only through kings and stories.

4. Origin Narrative of Vijayanagara . Vidyaranya Narrative

Time: 15 mins
Location: Same seated space on Hemakuta Hill

Guide narrative:
The guide narrates the Vidyaranya, Harihara-Bukka, and hare-and-hound origin story. The focus is not simply the story itself, but how origin narratives create legitimacy, sacred authority, and political meaning. Students should compare the previous evidence-based explanation with this symbolic narrative and understand that both reveal different things about Vijayanagara.

Facilitator action:

  • Keep students seated and settled. This section is narration-heavy.
  • Watch for attention drop and use prompts only if needed.

Facilitator prompts (ask if needed):

  • Why do you think this became the most popular origin story of Vijayanagara? (at the end)
  • How does Vidyaranya’s presence make the founding of Vijayanagara feel divinely approved? (at the end)
  • Which explanation helps us understand the capital better: the geographical explanation, the origin story, or both together?

Must land: The origin story is not just a legend. It is political communication that gives Vijayanagara sacred authority and legitimacy.

5. Temples as Community, Economic, and Political Centers

Time: 15 mins
Location: Near Mula Virupaksha, with a bird’s-eye view of Virupaksha Temple and the surrounding landscape

Guide narrative:
The guide uses the view of Virupaksha and its surroundings to explain temples as large social institutions. Students should understand that temples were not only places of worship, but also centers of economy, administration, charity, law, community, festivals, and political power. Virupaksha should be seen as part of a larger sacred, social, and urban system.

Facilitator action:

  • Choose a safe viewing position with enough space for the group.
  • Keep students away from edges, slopes, and uneven stone surfaces.

Facilitator prompts (ask if needed):

  • Why would markets form in front of major temples?
  • What roles did temples play beyond worship?
  • How are traders, farmers, priests, pilgrims, and local communities all connected through the temple?

Must land: Temples in medieval South India were social, economic, political, and sacred institutions, not only religious buildings.

6. Sunset Close

Time: 30 mins
Location: Hemakuta sunset viewpoint

Guide narrative:
This is a quiet reflective close, not another information-heavy section. Students should sit with the landscape after moving through scale, sculpture, geography, origin stories, and temple institutions. The close should help them experience Hampi as a sacred, political, historical, artistic, geographical, and living landscape.

Facilitator action:

  • Move students safely to the sunset viewpoint.
  • Seat the group with clear boundaries. No running, climbing, or wandering.
  • Conduct headcount before exiting.

Facilitator prompts (ask if needed):

  • What is one thing you noticed in the landscape today that you had not noticed before?
  • What is one story or idea that changed how you see Hampi?
  • What is one question you are leaving with?

Must land: Students should leave with the sense that Hampi is not just a ruin site. It is a layered landscape shaped by geography, sacred memory, political power, community life, and living tradition.

Core Pathway Has Landed If

Students can say that the Sacred Area is more than a cluster of temples:

  • It combines sacred geography, landscape, sculpture, origin stories, political authority, and living cultural memory.
  • Sasivekalu Ganesha shows how stone, craft, proportion, symbolism, and emotion work together.
  • Hemakuta helps explain why Vijayanagara chose this landscape as a capital.
  • The Vidyaranya narrative shows how origin stories create legitimacy and sacred authority.
  • Virupaksha and the surrounding temple landscape show that temples were social, economic, political, and sacred institutions.
  • The sunset close helps students experience Hampi as layered, reflective, and alive.
