Sunrise Trek - Facilitator Card

MATANGA HILL SUNRISE TREK: FACILITATOR CARD

Use During Live Module

Live execution card. Use only for safety, movement, timing, warm-up, prompts and pathway decisions. For depth, read the master playbook before the trip.

Snapshot

Duration: Short Pathway: approximately 4.5 hours | Long Pathway: approximately 5.5 hours

Departure: 5:00 AM from hotel

Group: 1 guide + 2 to 3 facilitators per approximately 30 students

Grade: 6 to 8

Route: Hotel → Trek Base (Kadalekalu Ganesha Temple) → Ascent via Virupaksha side → Mid-way rest → Summit (Veerabhadra Temple) → Descent via Achyutaraya side → Short: return to hotel | Long: boulder jumping (approximately 300 metres from Achyutaraya Temple) → hotel

Use when: Both pathways run the full summit experience. Choose the Long Pathway only if group energy, time and safety conditions allow boulder jumping after descent.

Start Checks

Pathway: Confirm Short or Long Pathway before departure from hotel.

Group: Check water bottles and closed-toe shoes. Complete fatigue and health checks. Identify students who need extra supervision.

Adults: Brief teachers. Place one at the front with the Lead Facilitator and one at the rear with the closing Facilitator.

Formation: Lead Facilitator at front. One Facilitator mid-group. One Facilitator at rear. Guide moves freely. No student ahead of the lead. No student behind the rear.

Risk points: Pre-dawn movement, uneven rocky trail, stepped ascent, summit temple conduct, descent steepness and boulder-jumping surfaces for the Long Pathway.

Lost student protocol: Student stops and stays in place. Student does not descend alone. A Facilitator returns to locate them.

Medical: First-aid kit with the Lead Facilitator. If a student experiences dizziness, breathlessness or nausea, stop the group immediately.

Rules to Repeat as Needed

“Stay together. No student moves ahead of the Lead Facilitator.”

“The pace is set by the slowest student. No racing.”

“Watch every step. The terrain is uneven. No running on the trail.”

“Hydrate regularly. Do not wait until you are thirsty.”

“At the summit, respect the temple. No shouting, climbing on the structure, or touching idols or sacred surfaces.”

“Descend more slowly than you ascended. Do not rush downhill.”

“During boulder jumping, wait for Facilitator clearance before every jump. One at a time. Land with bent knees.”

Section 1. Hotel Briefing and Departure

Section Details

Time: 10 mins

Facilitator Instructions

  • Complete a full headcount before leaving the hotel.
  • Confirm water and footwear.
  • Set the group formation.
  • Brief students on walking conduct during pre-dawn movement.
  • Use phone torches if visibility is low.
  • Set the tone. This morning is unusual and worth being present for.

Script

“We move together as one group. No one moves ahead of me. No one falls behind the last Facilitator. If you feel unwell at any point, let the Facilitators or teachers know immediately.”

Section 2. Trek Base: Warm-Up and Trek Rules

Section Details

Time: 10 to 15 mins

Location: Near Kadalekalu Ganesha Temple, at the base of the Virupaksha-side ascent

Facilitator Instructions

  • The Facilitator leads the full warm-up. The Guide does not deliver content here.

Warm-Up Sequence

Lead with energy and move through this briskly.

  • Neck rolls: 30 secs
  • Shoulder rolls: 30 secs
  • Wrist and ankle circles: 30 secs each
  • Hip circles: 30 secs
  • Knee circles: 30 secs
  • Dynamic leg swings: 1 min per leg
  • Squats: 5 to 8 reps
  • Calf raises: 10 reps

Script

After the warm-up, state the trek rules clearly.

“One: stay in the group. Two: watch your step. Three: pace yourself. This is not a race.”

Complete a headcount before the ascent begins.

Section 3. Trek to Matanga: The Ascent

Section Details

Time: 25 to 35 mins

Facilitator Instructions

  • The Lead Facilitator sets the pace at the front, governed by the slowest student.
  • The mid-group Facilitator monitors energy and spacing.
  • The rear Facilitator ensures no one is left behind.
  • Do not rush. If a student is struggling, slow the group. Never leave a student behind.
  • The Guide may offer short spoken observations while climbing. Facilitators do not need to add content here. Focus entirely on movement and safety.

Watch For

  • Students racing ahead.
  • Large gaps forming in the line.
  • Students sitting down without flagging distress.
  • Slipping on uneven steps.

Section 4. Mid-Way Rest

Section Details

Time: 5 to 8 mins

Facilitator Instructions

  • Call the stop.
  • Enforce hydration. Students should take a proper drink, not just a sip.
  • Complete a quiet headcount.
  • Draw attention to the view opening up around the group.

Script

“Drink water. Take a few proper gulps. Look west. You can see the Virupaksha Temple tower from here. Look east. The Achyutaraya Temple is visible below. We are almost there.”

Check student energy. If any student is struggling significantly, assess whether to continue or have a Facilitator stay with them at a safe point.

Section 5. Summit: Mythology Session

Section Details

Time: 15 to 20 mins

Guide Content

Cover Sage Matanga, the curse on Vali, Sugriva's exile, Hanuman, Rama and Lakshmana, and the Kishkindha alliance. Connect all of it to the visible landscape from the summit.

Facilitator Instructions

  • Settle the group before the Guide begins.
  • Seat students on stable, flat surfaces. Do not allow them to perch on boulders or the temple structure.
  • Prevent touching of the temple.
  • Keep the group compact and facing the Guide.

Watch For

  • Wandering or distraction.
  • Sitting on temple walls.
  • Students moving towards edges.

Facilitator Prompts, Ask If Needed

  • “What does it feel like to stand in a place that appears in a story as ancient as the Ramayana?”
  • “Can you identify hills, the river or landmarks around us that match the story we just heard?”
  • “Why do you think people told stories about a sacred monkey kingdom in a landscape that looks exactly like this one?”
  • “If you had to find a place of safety where no enemy could follow, what kind of landscape would you choose?”

Section 6. Summit: History Session

Section Details

Time: 15 to 20 mins

Guide Content

Cover the founding of Vijayanagara, the strategic use of the hills and river, Matanga Hill as a defensive and sacred position, the Veerabhadra Temple and its inscriptions, the city visible from the summit, and Hampi as the second-largest city in the world by 1500 CE.

Facilitator Instructions

  • Keep students oriented towards the Guide and the landscape being discussed.
  • Encourage students to look in the directions the Guide indicates: west for Virupaksha, east for Achyutaraya and north for the river.

Watch For

  • Restlessness between sessions.
  • Students sitting on temple walls.
  • Heat affecting attention.

Quick Re-Engagement

If energy dips between the mythology and history sessions, use this prompt.

“Before the Guide continues, look east and find the Achyutaraya Temple ruins. Can you spot them?”

Facilitator Prompts, Ask If Needed

  • “Looking at this landscape, what made Hampi difficult to attack?”
  • “Can you identify bazaar streets, temple towers or the river from up here?”
  • “Why do you think kings combined sacred places with defensive positions?”
  • “Hampi was once the second-largest city in the world. Does that surprise you, looking at what you see today?”

Section 7. Summit: Geology Session

Section Details

Time: 15 to 20 mins

Facilitator Instructions

  • The Facilitator leads this session fully. The Guide does not deliver content here.
  • Keep the language simple and anchor everything to what students can physically see and touch around them.

Content to Cover

Age of Closepet Granite, approximately 2.5 billion years; the Dharwar Craton; how granite formed from magma underground; how boulders acquired their rounded shapes through deep weathering and the removal of grus; the concept of inselbergs; and the connection between geology, mythology and history.

Opening Script

“Place your hand on the boulder nearest to you. Feel the grain. What you are touching is approximately 2.5 billion years old, older than dinosaurs, older than the Himalayas, older than almost anything you have ever touched.”

Closing Script

“The reason this landscape looks the way it does, full of boulders and broken terrain, is the reason Sugriva hid here and the reason the Vijayanagara kings built here. Geology, mythology and history are the same story told in different languages.”

Facilitator Prompts, Ask If Needed

  • “If these rocks are 2.5 billion years old, how does that compare with when dinosaurs existed?”
  • “What do you think caused the boulders to become round instead of jagged?”
  • “How do you think this landscape influenced the people who lived here?”
  • “Can you think of other cities built in difficult terrain for similar defensive reasons?”
  • “If you were building a city and had to choose a landscape, what would you choose and why?”

Section 8. Descent via the Achyutaraya Side

Section Details

Time: 20 to 30 mins

Facilitator Instructions

  • The Lead Facilitator sets a slow, cautious pace at the front.
  • The Guide walks mid-group and may point out Sugriva Cave and Achyutaraya Temple as they come into view.
  • The rear Facilitator closes the group.
  • Strictly prohibit running during the descent. Downhill movement on uneven rock is harder on the knees than climbing.
  • Assist students who feel unsteady.
  • Complete a headcount at the base of the descent before any pathway split.

Pathway Confirmation

At the base, confirm the pathway.

Short Pathway: Use for the Short Pathway.

“Well done. We head back to the hotel now. Stay together.”

Long Pathway: Use for the Long Pathway.

“Well done. We walk approximately 300 metres to the boulder-jumping area. The same rules apply. Stay together and follow me.”

Section 9. Boulder Jumping, Long Pathway Only

Section Details

Time: 25 to 35 mins

Location: Approximately 300 metres from Achyutaraya Temple

Facilitator Instructions

  • Assess every jump surface before any student uses it.
  • Wet, mossy or visibly slippery surfaces are off-limits.
  • Signal clearance for each jump.
  • Allow one student at a time.
  • Prevent racing or crowding on boulder surfaces.

Safety Script

Deliver this before any jumping begins.

“I assess each spot first. You wait for my signal. No jump happens without my clearance. Land with bent knees. I will show you once. If you do not want to jump, that is completely fine. Watch from a flat, safe spot.”

Facilitator Instructions, Continued

  • Demonstrate a bent-knee landing before students begin.
  • Do not pressure students who choose not to jump. They should observe from a safe, flat surface.
  • The Guide assists with surface assessment and may make casual remarks connecting the boulders to the geology and Ramayana sessions from the summit.

Watch For

  • Students attempting jumps without clearance.
  • Crowding on narrow surfaces.
  • Racing.
  • Pushing near edges.

Trek Has Landed If

Students can say:

  • Matanga Hill is sacred in the Ramayana. Sugriva hid here because Vali could not follow.
  • The Veerabhadra Temple at the top has inscription records going back to at least 1199 CE.
  • The Vijayanagara founders chose this landscape deliberately. The hills and river were natural defences.
  • The granite boulders are approximately 2.5 billion years old and acquired their rounded shapes through deep weathering over millions of years.
  • Geology, mythology and history are three layers of the same landscape.
  • Boulder-jumping safety rules were followed independently and confidently.
  • Students can explain in their own words how the landscape shaped both the stories told about it and the empire built within it.
