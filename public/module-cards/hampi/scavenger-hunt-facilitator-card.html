Scavenger Hunt - Facilitator Card

Quick Summary

Raya’s Trail is a teacher-supervised, scored scavenger hunt inside the Royal Enclosure. Students use the worksheet to locate structures, observe physical evidence, capture proof through teacher-taken photos, and infer the possible functions of different structures.

The activity is part of the larger archaeology theme of the program. Its core learning sequence is:

Observation → Evidence Gathering → Inference → Interpretation

Duration

Group Structure

  • Group size: 6 to 8 students per team
  • Each group must have: 1 assigned teacher
  • Teacher role: Group supervision, photo evidence, safety, cohesion
  • Student role: Read clues, solve tasks, discuss, write answers
  • Facilitator role: Brief, station strategically, clarify doubts, monitor safety, score, debrief

Before Students Arrive

Confirm:

  • Group-wise printed worksheets are ready.
  • Each worksheet has the same challenges but a different page order.
  • One teacher is assigned to each group.
  • Facilitator station points are decided.
  • High-supervision groups are identified.
  • Briefing point outside the Royal Enclosure is clear.
  • Entry gate for staggered release is clear.
  • Final assembly / scoring point is marked on the map.
  • First-aid and water plan are ready.

Core Learning Intent

Students should understand that an archaeological site is not just a collection of ruins. It is a field of evidence.

They should practice:

  • Observing structure, layout, design, material, placement, carvings, water channels, tanks, gateways, platforms, and chambers.
  • Distinguishing between observation and inference.
  • Inferring possible activities and functions from physical evidence.
  • Supporting answers using the site, worksheet, map, signboards, and photographs.
  • Working as a team to solve location-based and evidence-based problems.

Opening Script

“Today, you are exploring the Royal Enclosure through Raya’s Trail.

This is a scavenger hunt, but it is not only about finding things quickly. Your job is to read the site like investigators. Every platform, tank, aqueduct, doorway, staircase, carving, drain, stone block, and chamber can become evidence.

This activity is connected to the archaeology work we are doing in the program. Archaeologists do not always begin with complete answers. They begin by observing what remains, gathering evidence, and making careful inferences.”

Archaeology Frame

“As you move through the Royal Enclosure, keep asking yourselves:

  • What do we see?
  • What evidence can we capture?
  • What might this structure have been used for?
  • What does the design, layout, or material suggest?
  • What can we infer confidently?
  • What remains uncertain?

By the end of this activity, you should have a better sense of how people used this part of Vijayanagara’s citadel. You will see clues about festivals, water systems, movement control, administration, and royal life.”

Rules and Safety Script

“Stay with your assigned teacher at all times. No one moves alone.

Do not run. Do not climb on monuments, walls, platforms, boulders, tanks, chambers, or railings.

Do not lean over tank edges, drains, or the underground chamber.

Do not touch, scratch, or lean on carvings or fragile structures.

If you are stuck, ask a stationed facilitator.

At the end, every group must assemble at the point marked on your map.”

Teacher Role Script

“Teachers, please stay with your assigned group throughout the activity.

Your role is to keep the group together, capture the required photo evidence, and ensure students work collaboratively and safely.

Please do not solve the clues for them. Let the students lead the thinking. Step in for safety, group management, or if the group is completely stuck.”

Scoring Script

“Each challenge carries points. Points are awarded for correct written answers and correct photo evidence.

If a photo is unclear, your group may be asked to explain what it shows.

Points can be lost for unsafe behaviour, separating from the group, climbing, damaging the site, or not following instructions.

The team with the highest score wins.”

Worksheet Handover

  1. Call one group at a time.
  2. Confirm assigned teacher.
  3. Hand over the correct group worksheet.
  4. Confirm group name / number.
  5. Tell students to begin with the first challenge page.
  6. Hold the group until release.

Facilitator line:

“Your worksheet has your group’s order. Begin with the first challenge page. Stay with your teacher. Students solve and write. Teachers capture photos.”

Staggered Release

Release groups one at a time through the selected entrance.

Before releasing each group, confirm:

  • Teacher is with the group.
  • Worksheet is in hand.
  • Students know their first challenge.
  • Group understands the assembly point.

Facilitator line:

“Check your first challenge. Stay together. Move safely. You may begin.”

Assembly and Scoring

At the marked assembly point:

  1. Count students and confirm all groups have returned.
  2. Ask teachers to keep photo evidence ready.
  3. Ask groups to stop editing answers once scoring begins.
  4. Call one group at a time.
  5. Check written answers.
  6. Check required photos.
  7. Award points and record total score.
  8. Ask for explanation if a photo is unclear.
  9. Announce the winner after all groups are scored.

Scoring line:

“We are not only checking whether you found the place. We are checking whether you found the right evidence.”

Non-Negotiables

  • Students stay with their teacher and group.
  • No running.
  • No climbing.
  • No leaning over tanks, drains, or chambers.
  • No touching or scratching carvings.
  • Teachers capture photos. Students do not scatter for photos.
  • All groups return to the marked assembly point.
  • Safety and site protection take priority over task completion.

Materials Required

  • Group-wise printed Raya’s Trail worksheets
  • Pencils or pens
  • Clipboards, if available
  • Teacher phone / camera for photo evidence
  • First-aid kit
  • Drinking water access plan
