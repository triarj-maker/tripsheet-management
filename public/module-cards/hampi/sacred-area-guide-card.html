Section 1:  Introduction to Sacred Area

Location: Entrance gate to near Saasivekalu Ganesha

  • Vijayanagara was spread across roughly 250 sq km at its peak, including suburbs. Around 40 sq km of the core city is protected as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
  • The core city has several monument clusters with different functions. - The Sacred Area is one such cluster.
  • Why do you think this place has that name: Sacred Area?
  • It is called the Sacred Area because of its religious and spiritual significance and the large number of shrines, temples, and sacred spaces.
  • How many of you have read or heard about the Vijayanagara Empire?
  • Vijayanagara was one of the largest and wealthiest city-states of its time.
  • Does anyone know which were two of the largest city-states in the world during the 1500s?
  • Vijayanagara reached peak during pre-modern era, important time in History.
  • Four major temple complexes to identify: Achyutaraya, Krishna, Virupaksha, Vittala.

Section 2:  Saasivekalu Ganesha

Location: Saasivekalu Ganesha

  • If you look around, there is one material that is everywhere. Under your feet, in the hills, fort walls, pillars, and roofs. What is this material?
  • Granite is the dominant material in the local landscape. Granite is abundant but difficult to work with because it is hard, dense, and heavy.
  • Shilpa Shastra guided sculpture through rules of proportion, symbolism, and emotional effect.
  • Tala system: one Tala is based on the size of the deity’s head.
  • Ganesha in teen Tala / three Talas creates approachability.
  • Rasa: Indian art aims to evoke a particular emotional experience.

Section 3: Why Vijayanagara Chose this Location

Location: Double Storey Mantapa, Seated in Shade

  • What do you see in the landscape that might make this a good place for a capital city?
  • Boulder hills created natural defense. An invading army would find it difficult to move through this terrain because of the rocks, hills, slopes, and narrow routes.
  • Granite provided abundant building material. The same stone seen at Sasivekalu Ganesha is available everywhere here and was used for walls, temples, platforms, mandapas, fortifications, and sculptures.
  • The existing sacred landscape helped rulers gain local support and legitimacy. There were older shrines, pilgrimage traditions, and sacred associations here before Vijayanagara became an empire.
  • The founding families had connections to nearby regions such as Anegundi and Kampli. This was part of their political and social world, not an unknown place.
  • The northern frontier mattered because Vijayanagara’s major enemies were to the north. Being close to the frontier allowed faster military response instead of hiding deep in the south.
  • The Tungabhadra River provided water, irrigation, agriculture, sanitation, and movement of goods.
  • Capital location is shaped by many factors, not a single reason: defense, building material, sacred importance, local political connections, military strategy, and access to water.

Section 4: Origin Narrative of Vijayanagara . Vidyaranya Narrative

Location: Double Storey Mantapa, Seated in Shade

  • This is the most popular origin story of Vijayanagara. It appears in later literature, roughly 100 to 150 years after the empire was founded.
  • Stories like this usually do not become popular by accident. If a kingdom’s origin story is repeated in courtly literature, temple traditions, and later memory, it often reflects what the ruling family or its descendants wanted people to remember.
  • The story speaks about Harihara and Bukka, the two founding figures of Vijayanagara. (narrate the whole story)
  • According to the story, Harihara and Bukka accepted this advice and decided that this was the right place to build their capital. Vidyaranya was appointed as the spiritual and political guide to the founders.
  • In some versions of the larger tradition, this is framed as a divine mission. These young rulers are not simply choosing a location for practical reasons. They are being guided toward a sacred purpose: to establish a kingdom that would protect dharma and the dharmic way of life.
  • The story carries subtle political messaging, especially in the context of powerful Islamic sultanates to the north, first the Delhi Sultanate and later the Deccan Sultanates.
  • Royal origin stories help rulers legitimize power. Kings and dynasties often claim divine approval to make their rule appear chosen, blessed, and above ordinary authority.
  • The historical explanation helps us understand practical reasons: defense, water, stone, politics, and location. The origin story helps us understand how rulers wanted people to imagine the founding of the empire.
  • Which explanation helps us understand the capital better: the geographical explanation, the origin story, or both together?

Section 5: Pampa Mahatmya and Sacred Landscape (Optional)

Location: Top of Hemakuta Hill, overlooking Virupaksha from top

  • This section is optional. Use it for groups with interest in mythology, religion, sacred geography, or deeper cultural context.
  • Hemakuta and Virupaksha were important before Vijayanagara. Pampa and Virupaksha are central to how this place becomes Hampi / Pampakshetra.
  • Pampa Mahatmya is a Stala Purana: a sacred place-text or temple-origin narrative. Stala Puranas preserve sacred geography, rituals, animals, people, traditions, and stories.
  • There are two perspectives here: the sacred/literary narrative and the archaeological/historical sequence.
  • The sacred story begins with Sati, Daksha, Shiva’s grief, the Tandava, and the creation of Shakti Peethas. The Pampa story begins where the Sati story ends.
  • According to the Pampa Mahatmya, Shiva comes to Hemakuta to meditate after grief and rage. He withdraws from the world, and cosmic balance is disturbed.
  • The place becomes Pampakshetra: the sacred field or place of Pampa. Pampa Sarovara is connected to her meditation and is mentioned in sacred literature.
  • The Pampa-Virupaksha wedding festival is still important in Hampi. The festival involves different communities: fishermen, flower decorators, farmers, priests, and others.
  • Archaeologically, Pampa may represent an older local or river goddess tradition. Pampa is referred to in Chalukyan-period evidence.
  • Later, Vira Shaivism connects Pampa with Shiva as Virupaksha. Pampa Mahatmya helps create a larger sacred universe around the landscape.
  • Hampi is a layered religious landscape where older traditions are absorbed, reinterpreted, and connected across centuries.

Section 6: Temples as Community, Economic, and Political Centers  (Optional)

Location: Top of Hemakuta Hill, overlooking Virupaksha from top

  • This section is optional. Use it for mixed nationality groups or students with less context to Indian mythology or religion.
  • From near Mula Virupaksha, use the bird’s-eye view of Virupaksha Temple to show that temples were part of a larger urban system.
  • Temples in South India were not only places of worship. They were centers of community, economy, administration, charity, law, and power.
  • Why would markets form in front of major temples?
  • Major temples had markets in front of them. These markets brought traders, pilgrims, artisans, and visitors. Trade created wealth and could be taxed.
  • What roles did temples play beyond worship?
  • Temples had large kitchens and fed people. Temples and temple-linked communities could also help resolve local disputes.
  • Virupaksha should be understood as the heart of a social, economic, and sacred system, not only as a shrine.
